# Email Frameworks

Frameworks for cold outreach copy. Use these to structure messages, not to copy word-for-word.

---

## PAS - Problem, Agitate, Solution

Best for: First message when the pain is obvious and specific.

Structure:
1. Name the problem (they recognize it)
2. Make it feel real (what it's costing them)
3. Offer a solution or hint at it

Example:
```
Subject: Your website is losing leads to [Competitor]

[Name],

[Competitor] ranks above you for "[search term]" in Google right now.
Every day that stays true, leads who are searching for what you offer are
finding them instead.

We've helped 3 companies in [industry] close that gap in under 90 days.

Worth a quick call to show you how?

[Sender]
```

When to use: When you found a clear, specific problem in research. Do not use for vague pain.

---

## BAB - Before, After, Bridge

Best for: Follow-up messages or when leading with aspiration.

Structure:
1. Before state (current situation - their reality)
2. After state (what changes - the outcome they want)
3. Bridge (how you get there)

Example:
```
Subject: From scattered leads to a predictable pipeline

[Name],

Right now your leads come from referrals and word of mouth - great when
it works, stressful when it dries up.

Imagine having a system that generates 8-12 qualified leads per month
without depending on who you know.

That's what we built for [similar company] in 4 months.

15 minutes to show you the setup?
```

When to use: When the prospect's current state is limiting and the after state is aspirational and specific.

## One-liner observation

Best for: Hyper-short first messages. High volume, cold list.

Structure:
One observation + one question. Nothing else.

Example:
```
Subject: [Company] + [specific thing you noticed]

[Name],

Noticed your Google Ads are running but there's no conversion tracking
on your site - meaning you're spending without knowing what's working.

Is that something you're aware of?

[Sender]
```

When to use: When you found one very specific technical observation. Works for agency, dev, or tool outreach.

---

## The Helpful Intro

Best for: Warm-ish lists or LinkedIn outreach where you want to lead with value.

Structure:
Give something useful before asking for anything.

Example:
```
Subject: Quick thing I found about [Company]

[Name],

I was looking at your site and noticed [Company] isn't showing up for
"[keyword]" in [city] - [Competitor A] and [Competitor B] are.

Attached is a quick breakdown of what they're doing differently.

No ask - just thought it might be useful.

[Sender]
```

When to use: When building credibility matters more than speed. Slower but higher quality conversations.

---

## The Referral Frame

Best for: When you have any shared connection, worked with a competitor, or can reference a relevant case study.

Structure:
Open with the connection or reference before making an ask.

Example:
```
Subject: [Mutual contact] suggested I reach out

[Name],

[Mutual] mentioned you're looking at scaling your paid acquisition this
quarter.

We just helped [similar company in same industry] go from [X] to [Y] in [Z] months.

Happy to share exactly what we did - 20 minutes?

[Sender]
```

When to use: Only when the reference is real. Never fabricate shared connections.

## Follow-up frameworks

### The Value-Add Follow-up

Give something useful in the follow-up. No asking, just delivering.

Example:
```
[Name],

Following up on my last message. Thought you might find this useful:

[Relevant stat / article / observation specific to their situation]

Still happy to chat if the timing is right.

[Sender]
```

### The Soft Reframe

Slightly different angle on the same offer.

Example:
```
[Name],

I realize my last message was focused on [X]. Wanted to come back with a
different angle: [Y].

Same offer - 15 minutes. Would this angle be more relevant?

[Sender]
```

### The Break-up

Closes the loop. Often gets the highest reply rate of the sequence.

Example:
```
[Name],

Closing this thread since I haven't heard back. Completely understand - timing is everything.

If you ever want help with [specific thing you'd have solved], feel free to reach out.

[Sender]
```

## Subject line formulas

Best performing subject line types for agency cold outreach:

**Direct observation:**
- "[Company] + [specific thing noticed]"
- "Quick thing about [Company]'s [website/ads/SEO]"

**Question:**
- "Are you tracking [specific conversion]?"
- "Is your [Meta Pixel / GA4 / ads] set up correctly?"

**Name + topic:**
- "[Name] - [topic]"
- "[Company] + [your company]"

**Forward-style (higher open rate, use sparingly):**
- "Re: [related to something on their site]"
- "Following up on [Company]'s [ad campaign/SEO strategy]"

**Avoid:**
- "Quick question" (overused)
- "I wanted to reach out" (filler)
- "Partnering opportunities" (sounds like spam)
- All caps or excessive punctuation
- Emojis in B2B subject lines (exception: some B2C markets)

## Length guidelines

| Message | Max words | Rule |
|---------|-----------|------|
| First outreach | 80 | Every word must earn its place |
| Follow-up 1 | 60 | Lead with value, end with soft ask |
| Follow-up 2 | 50 | One new angle, one ask |
| Break-up | 40 | Close the loop, keep the door open |

Shorter is better. If you cannot cut it, cut harder.

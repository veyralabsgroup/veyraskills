# Proposal Structure

Format, tone rules, and section order for agency proposals generated in Phase 5.

---

## Core Principle

A proposal is not a report. A report describes. A proposal persuades.

The difference:
- Report: "Your website is missing a meta description."
- Proposal: "Your current search results show no description below your site name, which reduces click-through rate. This is a 1-day fix."

Every sentence in the proposal should answer one of:
- What is the problem?
- Why does it matter (business impact)?
- What does the agency do about it?
- What does the client get?

---

## Proposal Sections

### 1. Opening (2-3 sentences, no header)

Start with their situation, not your agency. First sentence = what you found after looking at their business. No "we are delighted to present" or similar.

Good:
> After reviewing [Company], we found 3 areas where the website is currently leaving leads on the table. This proposal focuses on the highest-impact changes.

Bad:
> [Agency] is a full-service digital marketing agency with 10 years of experience serving businesses like yours.

### 2. What We Found

3-4 specific problems, each with:
- Problem name (short, plain language)
- One sentence describing what was found (evidence-based, not opinion)
- One sentence on business impact

Format:
```
**[Problem Name]**
[What we found - specific, observable]
[Why it matters - business impact]
```

Example:
```
**No conversion tracking installed**
Your website has Google Analytics but no goal tracking or Google Ads conversion pixels.
Without this, it is impossible to know which traffic sources are generating leads or justify ad spend.
```

Rule: maximum 4 problems in this section. If you found 12 issues, pick the 4 that matter most. The rest can go in an appendix or be mentioned as "additional items to address."

### 3. Quick Wins

2-4 items that can be fixed in under a day, with visible result.

Format:
```
- [What] - [Expected result] - Estimated time: [X hours]
```

Example:
```
- Add meta descriptions to top 10 pages - improves search snippet CTR - 2 hours
- Replace generic "Submit" CTA with "Get a free quote" - increases form completions - 30 minutes
- Add 3 client testimonials to homepage - increases trust signals - 1 hour
```

Purpose: quick wins show the client they will see results immediately. Reduces "we'll think about it" friction.

### 4. Recommended Services

2-3 services maximum. Each includes:
- Service name
- What it solves (link to a problem from section 2)
- What the client gets (outcome, not activity)
- Indicative investment range

Format:
```
**[Service Name]**
Addresses: [Problem from section 2]
What you get: [Specific outcome - "10 SEO-optimized articles per month targeting [keywords]"]
Investment: [range or "from X€/month"]
```

Rule: do not list every service the agency offers. Recommend only what directly addresses the problems found. 2 targeted services > 8 generic ones.

### 5. Next Steps

One specific action. Not "contact us if interested."

Options:
- "Schedule a 30-minute call to walk through the findings and discuss priorities"
- "We can start with the Quick Wins this week at no cost as part of onboarding"
- "Reply to this email with your availability for a screen-share review"

The next step should be small and easy. Remove all friction.

## Tone Rules

**Use plain language**
No: "We leverage data-driven insights to optimize your digital touchpoints."
Yes: "We use analytics to find what's working and double down on it."

**Be specific**
No: "Your website needs better copy."
Yes: "Your homepage headline says 'Professional Solutions for Growing Businesses' - this could describe any company. We recommend rewriting it around your specific service and client type."

**Lead with their problem**
First half of every statement = their situation. Second half = what changes.

**Avoid agency jargon**
Banned words: leverage, synergy, KPIs, deliverables, best-in-class, holistic, robust, scalable, innovative, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art.

**Never use conditional tone for problems you found**
No: "Your website might be missing some conversion opportunities."
Yes: "Your homepage has no form or CTA above the fold. Visitors who do not scroll are leaving without a way to contact you."

**Match formality to the client**
- Local SMB: conversational, direct
- B2B mid-market: professional but plain
- Enterprise: formal structure, data-heavy

---

## Length

- Total proposal: 1-2 pages when printed (or a clean email)
- "What We Found" section: 4 items maximum
- "Recommended Services" section: 2-3 services maximum
- Never use bullet points inside bullet points
- Headers are visible - no subheadings below H2 level

Shorter proposals close faster. A 10-page proposal is a document. A 1-page proposal is a conversation starter.

---

## What to Include in an Appendix (optional)

If the client wants detail:
- Full list of all issues found with severity rating
- Competitor comparison table
- Keyword opportunities identified
- Technical checklist

The appendix is available if asked - it does not appear by default.

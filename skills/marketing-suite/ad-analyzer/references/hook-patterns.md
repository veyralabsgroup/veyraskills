# Hook Patterns

15 hook formulas for ad creative. A hook is the first line of copy or the headline that stops the scroll.

The hook determines whether someone reads the rest. If the hook fails, nothing else matters.

---

## The 15 Hook Formulas

### H1 - The Direct Question

Pattern: "Are you [negative situation]?"

Examples:
- "Are you still creating content that nobody sees?"
- "Are you spending on ads without knowing what converts?"
- "Tired of paying for leads that never show up?"

Works because: Creates immediate identification. Reader either says "yes, that's me" and keeps reading, or not.

Specificity test: Replace the brackets with something so specific that only your target customer would say yes.

---

### H2 - The Statistic Hook

Pattern: "[Specific number]% of [target] [surprising truth]"

Examples:
- "73% of ecommerce stores lose half their ad spend to untracked conversions."
- "8 out of 10 agencies lose clients in the first 90 days."
- "The average SMB wastes 11 hours a week on tasks that could take 30 minutes."

Works because: Numbers create credibility and make the problem concrete.

Avoid: made-up or unverifiable statistics. If you can't source it, don't use it.

---

### H3 - The Negative Hook

Pattern: "Stop [doing X]" or "Don't [do X until Y]"

Examples:
- "Stop posting on Instagram without a strategy."
- "Don't run Google Ads until you've done this."
- "Stop guessing which campaigns are working."

Works because: Pattern interrupts. Tells them they're doing something wrong. Creates curiosity.

Risk: Can feel aggressive. Works better for warm audiences or problems the target already suspects.

---

### H4 - The Before/After Hook

Pattern: "From [before state] to [after state] in [timeframe]"

Examples:
- "From 0 to 40 qualified leads per month in 90 days."
- "From Wix to a site that actually converts, in 3 weeks."
- "From posting twice a week to booking 5 discovery calls per month."

Works because: The transformation is immediate and tangible. Reader can see themselves in the after state.

Requirements: The before and after must be specific and believable. "More leads" is not an after state.

---

### H5 - The Story Hook

Pattern: "I [did something] until I [discovered/realized/learned X]"

Examples:
- "I spent €2,000 on ads every month and had no idea which ones were working."
- "I was losing 3 hours a day on reporting until I automated it."
- "I sent the same cold email to 400 people and got 3 replies."

Works because: Personal, relatable, implies a solution is coming.

Works best for: Founder-led brands, personal brands, case studies.

---

### H6 - The Curiosity Hook

Pattern: "The [surprising thing] most [target] get wrong about [topic]"

Examples:
- "The one thing most agencies get wrong about Meta Ads in 2025."
- "Why most landing pages lose 60% of visitors in the first 5 seconds."
- "The real reason your cold emails aren't getting replies."

Works because: Creates an information gap. Reader wants to know what they're missing.

Risk: Overused in the content space. Must be followed by genuinely surprising content.

---

### H7 - The Social Proof Hook

Pattern: "[Number] [target] [did X] because [specific reason]"

Examples:
- "500+ agencies switched their reporting tool in the last quarter."
- "12 ecommerce brands in Spain added €30k/month in revenue using this method."
- "These 3 SaaS companies went from 200 to 2,000 users in 60 days."

Works because: Social proof creates trust and urgency. Others are doing it - why aren't you?

Requirements: Numbers must be real and verifiable, or framed as case study data.

---

### H8 - The Problem Statement Hook

Pattern: "[Specific pain] is [cost they haven't calculated]"

Examples:
- "Every untracked conversion is money your competitors are taking from your ad budget."
- "An outdated website is costing you deals before your sales team even picks up the phone."
- "Your email list is losing 25% of its value every year you don't email them."

Works because: Makes the implicit cost explicit. Reader hadn't thought about it this way.

---

### H9 - The Warning Hook

Pattern: "If you're [doing X], [consequence]"

Examples:
- "If you're running retargeting without a pixel event setup, you're paying to reach everyone except buyers."
- "If your site isn't mobile-optimized, Google is penalizing your rankings right now."
- "If your Meta Ads aren't using advantage+ audiences, you're leaving performance on the table."

Works because: Creates urgency and specificity. Implies the reader is at risk right now.

---

### H10 - The Comparison Hook

Pattern: "[Your way] vs [the better way]"

Examples:
- "Manual reporting vs 3 minutes in a dashboard."
- "Guessing which ads work vs knowing in real time."
- "Cold emails with 2% reply rates vs warm outreach at 18%."

Works because: The contrast makes the improvement feel immediate and tangible.

---

### H11 - The Insider Hook

Pattern: "What [authority / insider] knows about [topic] that [target] doesn't"

Examples:
- "What performance agencies know about Meta Ads that most brands never figure out."
- "Why the top 10% of Shopify stores do their email differently."
- "The SEO tactic that big brands use and small businesses ignore."

Works because: Creates an in-group / out-group dynamic. Implies privileged knowledge.

---

### H12 - The Time Hook

Pattern: "[Result] in [surprisingly short time]"

Examples:
- "First 10 leads in 14 days."
- "A complete website audit in 15 minutes."
- "From idea to first sale in 30 days."

Works because: Time is the universal currency. Shorter time = lower perceived cost.

Risk: Must be believable. "Millionaire in 30 days" style claims destroy trust.

---

### H13 - The Identity Hook

Pattern: "For [specific type of person who identifies this way]"

Examples:
- "For agencies who are tired of manual reporting."
- "For founders who never want to think about ad tracking again."
- "For ecommerce brands ready to stop guessing."

Works because: Immediate self-identification. If you are that person, you keep reading.

---

### H14 - The Problem Recognition Hook

Pattern: "If [symptom], the real problem is [diagnosis]"

Examples:
- "If your ads have good CTR but no conversions, the problem is your landing page."
- "If you're getting traffic but no leads, the problem is usually the CTA."
- "If your email open rates are dropping, it's usually a deliverability issue, not subject lines."

Works because: Reframes a symptom as a diagnosable problem. Positions the brand as expert.

---

### H15 - The Specificity Hook

Pattern: "[Very specific detail that signals deep understanding of the target]"

Examples:
- "You've been posting 3 times a week for 6 months and your follower count is still under 800."
- "You installed GA4 but the migration from Universal Analytics broke your conversion goals."
- "Your Meta Ads are profitable on a 28-day attribution window but losing money on 7-day."

Works because: The specificity proves you understand the target's world. If they recognize themselves, they trust you immediately.

---

## Hook scoring criteria

When evaluating a hook, ask:
1. Is it specific enough that only the target customer nods?
2. Does it create a clear information gap or emotion?
3. Can you replace any word with a more specific word?
4. Is it under 15 words? (if not, can it be cut?)
5. Would you stop scrolling for this?

A hook that passes all 5 is worth testing.

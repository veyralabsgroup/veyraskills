# Creative Formulas

Ad copy frameworks. Apply these to structure full ad copy, not just hooks.

---

## PAS - Problem, Agitate, Solution

The most versatile framework. Works across platforms and formats.

Structure:
1. **Problem** - Name the specific problem the target has
2. **Agitate** - Make it feel real and costly (twist the knife)
3. **Solution** - Present the offer as the relief

Example (performance agency):
```
You're spending €3,000/month on Google Ads and you can't tell your client
which campaigns are actually generating leads. (Problem)

Every month you report "clicks" and "impressions" while they're asking
about ROI - and you're guessing. It's eroding trust. (Agitate)

We set up conversion tracking in 48 hours so you always know exactly which
ad, keyword, and audience is generating revenue. (Solution)

Book a call this week.
```

When to use: When the pain is clear and widely recognized. Best for B2B and service ads.

## AIDA - Attention, Interest, Desire, Action

Classic framework. Works well for longer ad copy and landing pages.

Structure:
1. **Attention** - Hook (see hook-patterns.md)
2. **Interest** - Expand on why this matters to them specifically
3. **Desire** - Make the outcome tangible and aspirational
4. **Action** - Clear, single CTA

Example (website redesign):
```
Your website is losing leads before your sales team picks up the phone. (Attention)

Most service businesses lose 60% of prospects at the website stage - not
because their service isn't good, but because the site doesn't answer
the one question every visitor has: "Can I trust these people?" (Interest)

Imagine sending a prospect to your website and having them say "This is
exactly what we were looking for." That's what a clear value prop,
real social proof, and a frictionless contact path does. (Desire)

See 3 sites we redesigned this year - and what changed after.
[See the case studies] (Action)
```

When to use: Awareness campaigns, longer content ads, educational content leading to an offer.

## BAB - Before, After, Bridge

Transformation-focused. Best for aspirational offers.

Structure:
1. **Before** - Their current reality (the unpleasant state)
2. **After** - The state they want (the desired outcome)
3. **Bridge** - How you get them from Before to After

Example (email marketing):
```
You have 2,400 contacts in your email list that you haven't emailed
in 6 months. (Before)

With the right welcome and nurture sequence, those same contacts could
be generating 30-50 inbound inquiries per month without any ad spend. (After)

We set up a 5-email sequence in 2 weeks that turns cold subscribers
into warm conversations. The last client we did this for had their
first inquiry within 48 hours of going live. (Bridge)

Want to see the sequence? [Book 20 minutes]
```

When to use: When the transformation is the main selling point. Good for email, SEO, content services where results take time.

## 4Ps - Picture, Promise, Prove, Push

Alternative to AIDA for direct response copy.

Structure:
1. **Picture** - Paint a vivid image of the situation or outcome
2. **Promise** - What changes with your offer
3. **Prove** - Evidence it works (case study, stat, testimonial)
4. **Push** - Urgency or reason to act now

Example (paid ads service):
```
Picture this: you open your ads dashboard Monday morning and you can
see exactly which campaigns generated leads last week, which ones
didn't, and what you're going to do differently. No guessing. (Picture)

That's what conversion tracking and a weekly reporting setup gives you.
Within 30 days you go from "we think ads are working" to "we know
exactly where each lead came from." (Promise)

We've set this up for 40+ agencies. Average result: 22% reduction in
cost-per-lead within the first 60 days by killing the campaigns
that weren't actually generating business. (Prove)

We're taking 3 new clients this month. [Book your call] (Push)
```

## One-sentence ad (for scroll-stopping short copy)

Pattern: [Painful reality] → [Offer] → [CTA]

Example:
```
You're ranking on page 2 for every keyword your competitors dominate on page 1.
We fix that. [Get the audit]
```

```
Every day your Meta Pixel isn't tracking conversions is data your competitors
are collecting and you're not. [Fix it in 48 hours]
```

Use case: Instagram Stories, short-form video overlay text, LinkedIn headline ads.

## Testimonial ad formula

When using a client quote as the main creative:

Structure:
1. Pull quote (the most specific and credible part)
2. Context (who said it, brief situation)
3. What you did
4. CTA

Example:
```
"We went from 12 leads per month to 47 in 60 days."

- [Client Name], [Company], [Role]

They came to us running Google Ads with no conversion tracking.
We set up the tracking, killed two campaigns that were generating
clicks but no leads, and doubled budget on the one that was.

[See how we did it]
```

Rule: Never fabricate quotes. If you do not have specific, real quotes, do not use this format.

## Video ad script structure (15-30 seconds)

Hook (0-3 seconds): One visual + one line of text that stops the scroll
Problem (3-8 seconds): Name the pain fast
Proof (8-18 seconds): Show the transformation or evidence
CTA (18-30 seconds): One action, one URL or button

Script example (15-second cut):
```
[Hook visual: dashboard showing "leads: 0" → "leads: 42"]
TEXT: "47 leads in 60 days from the same ad budget."

[VO/text]: Before: spending €2k/month with no idea what was working.
[VO/text]: After: tracked every lead back to its source, cut the waste,
doubled what was working.

[CTA screen]: "We'll set up your tracking in 48 hours.
[Book a call - link]"
```

## Platform-specific notes

### Meta Ads (Facebook / Instagram)
- First 3 seconds must hook on silent video
- Text overlay matters as much as audio
- 125 characters visible before "See more" in feed
- Social proof elements perform well in the visual
- Avoid stock imagery - real people, real results outperform

### Google Ads
- Headlines: 30 characters max, 3 headlines shown
- Descriptions: 90 characters max, 2 descriptions
- Include keyword in at least one headline
- CTA in one description line
- Use all ad extensions available

### LinkedIn Ads
- More expensive per click, justify with specific B2B targeting
- Introductory text: 150 characters visible before "See more"
- Image ads need to look native (not banner-ad style)
- Job title targeting > interest targeting for agency services

### Email (cold outreach ads / retargeting)
- Subject line = hook (under 8 words)
- Preview text = extension of subject line
- Under 150 words for cold outreach
- One CTA only
- Plain text often outperforms designed email for cold

## Ad copy quality checklist

Before finalizing any ad copy:

- [ ] Hook: specific enough that only the target customer nods?
- [ ] Pain: named clearly with real cost, not vague?
- [ ] Offer: specific outcome stated, not activity?
- [ ] Social proof: present? specific? real?
- [ ] CTA: one action only? active verb?
- [ ] Length: can any line be cut without losing meaning?
- [ ] Voice: does it sound like a human or a press release?
- [ ] Platform: does the length and format match where it runs?

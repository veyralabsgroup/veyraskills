# Question Banks

Question library for sales meetings, organized by pain type and meeting stage.

Rules:
- Questions should feel like curiosity, not interrogation
- Always tie questions to what was found in research ("I saw that...")
- One question at a time - never stack two questions in one message
- Listen for the answer before moving to the next question

---

## Opening questions (set the tone)

Purpose: confirm research, make them feel you prepared.

- "Before we dive in - I spent time looking at [Company] before this call. I noticed [specific observation]. Is that still accurate or has something changed?"
- "I saw you're on [platform] - is that still working well for you or is it something you've been thinking about?"
- "I noticed you're quite active on [channel] - has that been a good source of business for you?"
- "I saw you recently [launched/updated/announced X] - how's that been going?"

---

## Pain discovery questions (by type)

### Marketing visibility / leads

- "How are you currently getting most of your new clients?"
- "If I asked you where your last 5 clients came from, what would you say?"
- "Are you happy with the volume of leads you're getting, or is that something you're trying to grow?"
- "Have you tried [channel] before? What happened?"
- "When a potential client Googles [your service] in [your city], where do you come up?"

### Analytics and tracking

- "When you want to know if your marketing is working, what do you look at?"
- "Do you have a clear picture of where your leads are coming from, or is it still a bit fuzzy?"
- "If I asked you what your best-performing channel is, how confident would you be in the answer?"
- "Are you running any paid ads right now? How are you measuring the return?"

### Website and conversion

- "When you send a prospect to your website, what do you hope they do?"
- "Do you know roughly what percentage of visitors end up contacting you?"
- "Has anyone ever told you they visited your website and left without knowing what to do?"
- "When was the last time you redesigned or made significant changes to the site?"

### Social proof and closing

- "When a prospect is deciding between you and a competitor, what usually tips it in your favor?"
- "Do you have clients who would speak to a new prospect on your behalf?"
- "Do you have case studies or results you're proud of that you show to prospects?"
- "What do your best clients say about you? How do you capture that?"

### Growth and priorities

- "If you had to pick one thing in your marketing you'd fix first, what would it be?"
- "What does success look like for you in the next 12 months? How does marketing fit into that?"
- "Are you trying to grow, maintain, or shift focus right now?"
- "Is there a specific type of client or project you're trying to attract more of?"

### Budget and process

- "Do you have a budget set aside for marketing, or is that still to be defined?"
- "Have you worked with an agency before? What was that experience like?"
- "When you decide to invest in something like this, who else is involved in the decision?"
- "What's your timeline for making a decision on this?"

## Objection navigation

### "We tried that before and it didn't work"

Don't defend. Ask what specifically failed:
- "What specifically didn't work? Was it the channel, the execution, or the results?"
- "How long did you run it before deciding it wasn't working?"
- "Do you know what was being measured to call it a failure?"

### "We're not sure we have the budget"

Don't drop price. Anchor to outcomes:
- "What would it need to generate for it to be worth it?"
- "If we could show [specific result] in the first 90 days, would the budget be findable?"

### "We're talking to other agencies too"

Good signal - they're in buying mode:
- "That makes sense. What are the main things you're looking for in whoever you work with?"
- "What would make the decision clear for you?"

### "We need to think about it"

Find the real objection:
- "Of course. What's the main thing you'd need to feel clearer on?"
- "Is there something I haven't answered well enough?"

---

## Closing questions

- "Based on what we've talked about, does this feel like the right fit?"
- "What would need to happen next for this to move forward?"
- "If we sent you a proposal this week, is there a window in the next 2 weeks where you could review it together?"
- "Is there anything that would get in the way of moving forward in [timeframe]?"

---

## Questions to avoid

- "What keeps you up at night?" (overused, feels like a sales script)
- "What's your biggest challenge?" (too generic - they've heard it 100 times)
- "How would you describe your ideal solution?" (too abstract for most clients)
- Any question you already know the answer to from research (you prepared - use it)
- Multiple questions at once ("What's your budget and who else is involved and what's your timeline?")
